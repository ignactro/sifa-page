## Internet infrastructure and governance

I am interested in how the Internet is built, how it evolves, and how resilient it is to disruption. My current work on Internet resilience includes [RESILIOUS](https://cordis.europa.eu/project/id/101311731), an MSCA Doctoral Network, and I am also organising a [Dagstuhl workshop](https://www.dagstuhl.de/en/seminars/seminar-calendar/seminar-details/27201)  on the topic. I have also studied decentralised Internet infrastructure, including the InterPlanetary File System (IPFS) [[SIGCOMM 2022](https://doi.org/10.1145/3544216.3544232)].

I am interested both in Internet infrastructure and in its governance, including the development of the technical standards that underpin much of how it works. As part of this, I study how standards are made at the Internet Engineering Task Force (IETF) and the World Wide Web Consortium (W3C). This work started with the [SODESTREAM project](https://sodestream.github.io/) and continues through [RASP RG](https://datatracker.ietf.org/rg/rasprg/about/), which I co-founded and chair.

I also have a long-standing interest in the economics of the Internet. My PhD focused on how incentives and market structure shape Internet interconnection, including work on cooperative transit purchasing [[IEEE/ACM ToN 2014](https://doi.org/10.1109/TNET.2013.2278236)], remote peering [[CoNEXT 2014](https://doi.org/10.1145/2674005.2675013)], and  negotiation of interdomain connectivity [[HotOS 2015](https://www.usenix.org/conference/hotos15/workshop-program/presentation/castro)].

## Online platforms

I study how the architecture of online platforms affects how they are governed. I am particularly interested in decentralised platforms, where functions such as identity and moderation are distributed.

My work has examined the Fediverse (e.g., Mastodon and Pleroma), including migration and portability [[IMC 2024](https://doi.org/10.1145/3646547.3689027)] and decentralised moderation [[ICWSM 2024](https://doi.org/10.1609/icwsm.v18i1.31293)]. I have also studied content moderation in IPFS' decentralised infrastructure  [[USENIX Security 2024](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity24/presentation/sokoto)]. More recently I have examined the architecture of Bluesky, its growth and moderation [[IMC 2024](https://doi.org/10.1145/3646547.3688407)][[WWW 2026](https://doi.org/10.1145/3774904.3792106)]. More broadly, I am interested on content moderation and online harms, and on how platform architecture changes the way these problems can be addressed.

My work in this area has been funded through [AP4L](https://gtr.ukri.org/projects?ref=EP/W032473/1), [DSNmod](https://www.rephrain.ac.uk/), and the [Fediverse Observatory](https://www.rephrain.ac.uk/fedi-verse-impact-booster-award-project/).

## Online activity and society

I use online activity to study social and economic behaviour. This includes work on Nextdoor, where we examine how local online activity relates to neighbourhood inequality and crime [[ICWSM 2023](https://doi.org/10.1609/icwsm.v17i1.22155)]. I am currently extending this work by comparing what politicians discuss online and in Parliament with what their constituents discuss on Nextdoor.

I have supported this work with a grant studying the relationship between online discussions and crime is supported by the [Neighbourhood Signals](https://www.rephrain.ac.uk/neighbourhood-signals/) project, funded through the UK Government's Safer Streets framework.
